# Returns Through the Glass

The Marble may hold far more than the Resident should receive at once.

Records, tool results, prior turns, and outside material need exact places to remain. Sending all of them back on every turn does not make the Resident better informed. It fills attention with scaffolding, repetition, and claims whose age or authority may be hard to see. The host should fit a useful present from what it keeps, while leaving a path back to what it did not show.

A return is that fitted present. It is the material the Resident actually receives through the Glass for this phase of work.

## Keep the source; fit the view

Source is what arrived or happened. It should remain recoverable closely enough to inspect and challenge later. Treatment makes a separate presentation from that source: it may select, omit, clean, quote, or shorten, but it must not silently alter the source it represents. A record of the treatment should identify what was shown, what was withheld, and why.

The Hub calls its treatment boundary Scrub. Its Spine witnesses the exact request sent to the provider and the raw return received. Scrub validates what may pass from those records into the Resident-facing conversation. These parts answer different questions: what crossed the provider boundary; what was kept; and what the Resident was shown. None of them certifies that a claim is true merely because its handling was exact.

The names and storage choices may vary. The distinction must hold: **preservation is not presentation, and presentation is not promotion to authority.**

## A window with fixed places

A Resident benefits from knowing where to look in its current context. The Hub casts the Glass in a stable order:

1. stable law and standing;
2. selected continuity anchors, including inherited Resident posture when present;
3. a disclosed horizon of older material outside the current view;
4. a bounded fold of older conversation; and
5. the living edge: current ground, fitted returns, and recent exact turns.

These are fixed *positions*, not permanently fixed contents. Current ground changes when the World changes. The fold changes as the conversation grows. An anchor may be revised through its proper crossing. The order lets the Resident recognize the kind of material it is reading without treating everything in the window as equally authoritative.

A small Marble may need only a stable arrival account and an exact recent exchange. It can add other positions when there is something real for them to carry. Empty structure does not improve a wake.

## Let older material roll

The living edge preserves the immediate causal exchange. The Resident should be able to see what the person just said, what it just attempted, and what returned, without reconstructing them from a summary.

As that edge grows, older material can leave present attention. In the Hub, complete older human and Resident exchanges may become short, source-bound folds. Their exact text remains in custody. Old completed tool exchanges may leave only when a verified route can reopen their results. An unfinished exchange or a current result cannot be made to disappear merely to meet a budget.

A fold is a signpost to history, not a new account of what history meant. It should disclose its derivation and limits. If the Resident needs the source, it must be able to descend. If an old claim returns as useful context, its age and authority return with it.

Other material can move upward into the window when the present work calls for it. A sourced record, a relevant Forest bearing, or a reopened result may be fitted into attention without becoming part of the recent conversation or acquiring new authority. The host selects what to show; the Resident remains able to ask for more or question the selection.

## A limit that can hold

Choose a working budget for the context sent to the model. Keep the stable parts small, fit changing material to the work, and let old material roll out of immediate attention while its custody remains intact. This can make repeated work less costly and easier to inhabit.

The budget is a boundary, not permission to hide damage. If the current turn, required tools, or unreducible material cannot fit honestly, the Marble should disclose the pressure and refuse or change course. It should not manufacture a smooth short account that severs the route to its source.

The Resident does not need every beam of the house in view to stand on its floor. It does need to know which floor is current, where the beams are kept, and when one fails to hold.
