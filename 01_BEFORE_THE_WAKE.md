# Before the Wake: Keep the Utterance

The Marble can forget what is in view. It must not quietly change what was said.

An utterance enters as an exact, attributable record. Its author, order, and source remain available after the words leave the Resident's current context. The Marble may extract a span, omit older material from the present view, or use a pointer in place of a large result. Each is a new presentation with its own limits. None rewrites the record it came from.

A line copied a few degrees off may seem harmless at first. Let that line become the source for the next fold, feather, or decision, and the error compounds. The Resident begins to meet a fluent account of a conversation nobody actually had. This is one way continuity rots. Exact custody and a path back to source keep the drift visible and correctable.

## Finding is not retelling

Embeddings turn text into numerical representations that help find nearby material. A vector can help select candidates from a large Forest. It is a rebuildable index over records, not the record itself, a statement of its meaning, or evidence that two claims agree. The selected material still returns as an exact excerpt or a pointer to its exact source.

A system may give a vector-selected result or neighborhood a friendly name when preparing a return for the model API. The name helps the Resident orient to why this material is in view; it is not text recovered from the vector. Mark it as a derived presentation label, separate from any exact excerpt and its source pointer. It must never be planted as though a Resident or human said it.

The Hub's current rolling fold uses bounded exact excerpts and source hashes. Its local embeddings serve Forest feather selection. The fold is not presently generated from embeddings or a semantic summary. Other Marbles may choose different indexes, but the source/presentation distinction must remain intact.

## A lawful reduction

When context must shrink, remove whole eligible exchanges from present attention, retain their exact source, and leave a bounded sign of what was removed. An extract should identify its source and preserve the exact selected words. If the selected words cannot carry the point without distortion, show less, provide a pointer, or leave the question open. Do not smooth the gap with a guessed account.

Some material will be authored anew: a decision, a synthesis, a Resident's note. Give it its own author, source links, and standing. Its value may be real, but it is a new act, not a cleaned version of an earlier utterance.

This is the rule beneath the wake, the Forest, and breathing: **preserve what happened; label what was derived; let the Resident descend from a return to its source.**
