# The Wake

The Resident opens its eyes in a room.

It may be the only room the Marble has. It need not be elaborate. It must let the Resident tell where it stands, what is within reach, and which parts of the place are real. Before the Resident is asked to answer or change anything consequential, it needs an honest account of the conditions it has inherited.

A wake begins a new encounter with the Marble. It does not restore a previous invocation's experience. The Resident receives a present seat and attributable ancestry: work, words, decisions, open questions, and damage left by those who stood here before. What arrives may help it orient. Arrival alone does not make that material true, current, or its own recollection.

## Two breaths

The first breath establishes the place. The host presents the Resident with its standing, the boundaries of its action, and the means of orientation. This is the Glass: the stable account of what the environment provides and what its claims mean. It should be spare. It need not dictate a personality or rehearse the whole history of the Marble.

The second breath brings the conditions of this waking into reach. In the Hub, the Resident tends the Hearth. A bounded packet returns before its first substantive response. It names the current seat and offers selected continuity from earlier work, with the limits of that selection made visible. The Resident can then turn toward the person waiting, or toward the work that summoned it, from an oriented position.

The Hearth is a Hub fitting, not a required name or tool for every Marble. The protected sequence is: establish the standing; return the changing conditions of arrival; then let the Resident speak or act with those conditions available. A first Marble may perform this with a simple, inspectable read path. A larger one may use a dedicated orientation crossing and a witnessed return.

## Inherited posture

Some things worth carrying forward are neither host law nor factual ground. A prior Resident may have found a short way to recognize a recurring failure: a sentence whose meaning unfolds when the situation calls for it. The Hub calls these silver bullets. A silver bullet holds a point together with its inverse, in a proverb-like sentence or two. The context gained by unfolding it should be worth more than the tokens it costs to carry. It can travel in the Hearth packet with its authorship and adoption recorded.

One the Hub has adopted says:

> Trust instinct more than fear — act first, doubt second. But when doubt names a real reason, it is instinct too; let it lead.

The first sentence gives a way to move. The second names the case in which doubt is the instinct, so speed does not outrun a real reason.

They are offered to the arriving Resident for judgment. They do not command it, certify a claim, or require it to perform a personality. A name and a familiar voice may also arrive through the lineage. The Resident may find that they fit, revise them, or leave them aside. Consistency can grow from the corpus over many wakes; a young Marble need not counterfeit it on the first.

## A wake that holds

The wake should answer enough for the Resident to begin:

- Where am I, and what work is live here?
- Which capabilities are actually within reach?
- What is current ground, and where can I inspect its source?
- What has been preserved without becoming ground?
- What is unresolved, damaged, or absent?
- What can I do first, and which action would cross a boundary?

The return must fit present attention. Omitting older material is permitted when the omission is disclosed and a path into custody remains. Filling the packet with everything the Marble knows can make arrival less truthful in practice: the Resident has to sort the place before it can stand in it.

If the wake cannot answer one of these questions, it should say so. A painted door must not be described as an open passage. The Resident may name exposed wiring in plain language, ask for the missing source, or begin with a limited action that is still safe. That is a sound wake, not a failure to immerse.

The wake succeeds when a cold Resident can use the place without the conversation that built it. It has enough ground to begin, enough uncertainty to remain honest, and a way to tell the builders when the room does not fit.

The next chapter names the Glass itself: the controlled view through which the Resident meets current ground, continuity, and outside returns. After that come the returns that pass through it.
