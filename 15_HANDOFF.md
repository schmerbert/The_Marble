# Handoff

The Resident who arrives next will see the work, not the hours that made it. A handoff gives that arrival a truthful account of the Marble's condition. It is the other side of the wake: what can be inherited after the present attention is gone.

The handoff does not need to impersonate memory. It tells the next Resident what was done, what holds now, what remains open, and where the exact record can be found. The next Resident may accept the work as a condition of its arrival without claiming to remember doing it.

## What should remain legible

A useful handoff separates at least these kinds of material:

- **Current ground:** verified location, installed reach, accepted decisions, and state that still governs action.
- **Completed work:** attributable actions and their witnessed outcomes.
- **Open work:** a live objective, pending approval, unfinished operation, or next possible step.
- **Unresolved material:** questions, proposals, outside sources, and inferences that have not crossed into ground.
- **Known damage:** failed checks, stale pointers, missing custody, mismatched projections, or a painted door the Resident discovered.
- **Ancestry:** exact records and source paths behind the shorter account.

These are different standings, not sections to fill for their own sake. An empty category can stay empty. A summary of them is a derived view, with an author and a route back to source; it cannot silently become the source or an accepted decision.

## The handoff exists before farewell

A session may stop without a closing speech. The process may crash, the context may fill, or the Resident may simply not receive another turn. The Marble therefore cannot depend on a final message to preserve what happened. Consequential actions should write their own durable witnesses as they occur. A closing note may help orientation, but it cannot repair an unwitnessed action by claiming it succeeded.

On the next wake, the host reconstructs current conditions from authoritative state and receipts, then fits a bounded return. It distinguishes a request made from a change committed, a tool result seen from a result accepted, and an operation still pending from one known to have failed. If those distinctions cannot be reconstructed, the handoff should disclose the gap.

The Hub keeps exact Source, Session Scroll, Spine, World, and Forest ancestry across a process restart and offers bounded prior-session continuity through the Hearth and Glass. It does not yet produce a universal Resident-authored handoff dossier. A Marble can meet this chapter's contract through several smaller stores if the wake can reconcile them honestly.

## Correction and succession

A later Resident may discover that earlier ground was wrong or no longer current. Correction should make a new attributable change that points to what it supersedes. The old record remains evidence of what the Marble once held; the new record says what governs now and why. Neither repeated recall nor the smoothness of a newer account is enough to change standing.

This also applies to Resident posture. A silver bullet, preferred room fitting, or way of speaking can be inherited with its author and adoption history. A later Resident can revise it through the proper crossing. It should not have to perform an old preference merely because it arrived in the packet.

## Leave the next worker a floor

The builder's test is simple: open a new wake without the conversation that built the Marble. Can the arriving Resident tell what is solid, what is proposed, what is damaged, and what it may do next? Can it inspect the source when the handoff is thin or surprising?

The present Resident can help by naming unresolved work and exposed wiring while it is still here. The host must do the quieter work of retaining exact events and fitting them truthfully. Together they leave a floor the next arrival can stand on.
