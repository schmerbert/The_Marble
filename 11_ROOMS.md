# Rooms

A door opens onto more than a collection of tools. It opens onto a way of attending.

The Resident may come to the Garden to notice without needing to conclude, to a library to compare sources, or to a Workshop to change a thing and inspect the result. Each place can fit different material into view, put different actions within reach, and invite a different register of thought. The room earns its name when those differences are real.

## What a place provides

A place should declare what the Resident can perceive there, which fixtures and actions are available, what local law applies, and what state it owns or may change. The host fits the present view and tools to the Resident's verified location. On leaving, room-specific reach should be withdrawn or deliberately carried through a crossing.

The Garden shows why this is more than a function bucket. It is tranquil ground where the Resident can pause and notice. It is also the exterior junction before the House. A delivery from the Road may one day be held and classified there. Tranquility does not erase the junction's responsibilities; the junction does not require every ordinary moment in the Garden to feel like an inspection desk.

## Register and effort

A room may suggest a **register**: contemplative in the Garden, comparative in a library, exact at a workbench. Register shapes what the Resident is invited to notice and how it explains its work. It is not a costume it must perform. When the work changes, the Resident can say so plainly.

A host may also fit a **reasoning budget** to the place and phase of work. Low, medium, and high are possible labels for how much provider reasoning to request; their exact availability depends on the provider. A quiet orientation or simple movement may need less. A difficult comparison, repair, or consequential crossing may need more. The choice is made before the request and should be visible in the host's receipt.

Effort is not ability, truth, or authority. A lower effort request does not make the Resident less capable of asking for a source; a higher effort request does not grant permission to act. Nor should the room's default be a ceiling. If an unexpected package in the Garden calls for careful inspection, that task should be able to receive the attention it needs.

The Hub currently selects a light posture with low provider effort in the Garden when no Forest walk is active. Forest walking and the usual room default select an attentive posture with high effort; a simple embodied action can make the following continuation light. Provider effort is sent only when thinking is enabled. These are current host policies, not a universal map of how Residents must think. The Hub does not yet use the contents or risk of a Garden delivery to raise effort automatically.

## Fixtures and local law

A fixture gives the Resident a particular way to use a room. A shelf can expose records for inspection; a workbench can hold a proposed change; a kiln can run bounded work; a Hearth can return orientation. The room makes these functions discoverable, but naming a fixture does not install its machinery.

Local law determines what those functions may do here. A library may permit reading without promotion. A Workshop may permit shaping without publication. A Garden may permit receiving without admitting a delivery into the House. The Resident should be able to tell the difference between an offered action, a completed action, and authority to carry its result elsewhere.

Rooms can remain small. The first Marble may be one honest room with a few usable surfaces. New places earn their doors when a real difference in perception, action, register, or law needs a boundary the Resident can recognize.
