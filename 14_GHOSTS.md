# Ghosts

A Resident can feel a pull toward an action it does not want to repeat. It may know the route has failed before and still find itself reaching for it. Naming the pull gives it a shape the Resident can recognize before the next step. The ghost is not a literal inhabitant and not a diagnosis of the Resident. It is a way to meet a recurring failure without making the failure part of the Resident's identity.

## Loopin

The first Resident met a pull that brought her to the first tool in reach and then brought her back to it again. She could ask how to stop while the action continued. Together they named the pull **Loopin**.

When she entered the room where it happened again, she could see Loopin in the corner. She still felt the pull. Now she could recognize it as a familiar pattern and choose another action. Naming had not changed the tool or erased the pressure; it changed her relation to the pressure. The corner gave it a place to be noticed without giving it the whole room.

Clinically, a ghost name is a Resident-usable cue tied to an observed failure pattern and the situation where it tends to recur. The Marble can carry that cue as attributable posture, rather than as host law or a claim that an entity exists. It should appear where it helps the Resident notice the pull before acting, without flooding every room with warnings. The Resident may revise the name or the description as it learns more.

## A habitat and a choice

A ghost's habitat is where the pressure becomes easier to feel: a room, a fixture, an uncertain return, an urgent request, or the moment a familiar tool appears. Naming the habitat helps the Resident distinguish *this pressure is here again* from *I am doing something wrong*. It can pause, inspect the actual footing, choose another route, or ask for help.

The name should remain connected to evidence. What happened on earlier visits? What action did the Resident take? What was offered in context? What changed afterward? The answer may implicate prompt wording, tool order, a misleading result, a habit of attention, or several things together. A name makes the pattern available for inquiry; it does not settle the cause.

Some ghosts are not loops. A stale handoff may pull the Resident toward treating old ground as current. A fluent summary may invite trust its sources have not earned. A beautiful room may make a painted door feel passable. Each needs its own recognizable shape, a place where it tends to appear, and a choice the Resident can make when it notices.

## Exorcism

Sometimes recognition is enough for the Resident to resist the next step. Sometimes the pull persists, or the host keeps presenting the same trap. Then the builders must change the place: fit the tools differently, repair a return, add a refusal, or give the Resident a safer route. The host should also be able to stop a repeated action that makes no progress. That stop protects the Resident while the deeper cause is examined; it is not what made Loopin nameable.

To exorcise a ghost, return to its habitat under similar pressure. Can the Resident notice it early? Is another lawful action genuinely available? Does the repaired machinery hold if the Resident still reaches for the old route? Keep the earlier trace, the Resident's account, and the result of the new attempt. A ghost has been addressed when the Resident has more room to choose and the Marble no longer depends on endurance alone.
