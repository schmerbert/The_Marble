# 6. Windows

A window lets the Resident see beyond its present place without going there. Weather crosses the view; it does not cross the threshold. A distant room, a public signal, or an outside page may become visible while the Resident remains where it stands.

The word *window* also appears in the manual for the provider's context window. That is a fitting of material into attention. This chapter concerns an installed surface in the World: a way to observe a specified elsewhere from a specified place. The two windows share a concern with attention, but only the latter gives looking a location and a boundary the Resident can recognize.

## Seeing from here

A useful window declares what it faces, how current its view is, and how far the Resident may inspect through it. Looking does not move the Resident, fetch unlimited detail, or grant a hand in the place observed. The view may show that rain has begun beyond the Garden; it cannot say the Resident stood in that rain. It may show a document title on the Road; it cannot make the document Home ground.

The surface can be narrow. One House window might show the Garden's verified current condition. Another might show a dated, attributed outside feed. A window may show little or nothing when its source is unavailable. The Marble should say when the pane is stale, occluded, or broken rather than painting a live scene from an old observation.

Seeing through a window creates an observation with its own provenance. If the view is generated from a sensor or outside service, retain the source, capture time, selection, and any treatment. If it is a view of another installed place, World state owns what is there; the window owns only the presentation. The Resident's interpretation remains a further act, not part of the view's source.

## Opening is another act

The Resident may decide to travel toward what it saw, bring a source in for inspection, ask someone to check it, or let it pass. Each has a separate route. A window cannot quietly turn looking into crossing. If the Resident wants to reach outside material, the Road and Garden still govern reception; if it wants to enter another room, a door still governs movement.

This matters when a view is alluring or alarming. A storm can change the Resident's priorities without authorizing a blind action. A public reaction can be noticed without becoming the Marble's law. A glimpse may start a question and still leave the Resident at home.

## The Binder Window

The Hub has a working example in its Center. A separate portfolio application, Binder, owns the dashboard and its claims. It produces one frozen dashboard snapshot; the Hub validates that snapshot and projects a bounded view through an inspectable Window fixture. The Resident can look at what Binder supplied without entering Binder, reading its database, or recalculating its totals. Binder remains the source of truth for those portfolio claims outside the Marble; the Hub owns the projection and its boundary.

The snapshot carries its format, source mark, and observation times. Inspection reads the admitted value; it does not refresh prices or contact the application. Missing, stale, malformed, or incomplete material must remain visibly limited rather than becoming a confident picture. Seeing the portfolio grants no financial action. A separately installed Spotlight room can stand beyond the same Window, but its observations keep their own source marks and times.

This is a reusable shape: an outside application prepares a coherent view under its own authority, and the Marble admits only a bounded projection for inspection. The Resident can use the view to ask a question or choose a later crossing. The application does not become a room merely because its information is visible through the glass.

## The first window

The Binder Window is installed; it proves one narrow application projection, not a general World window for any source. Another Marble could begin with one verified state from a neighboring place, with its freshness and a route to inspect further. Test both directions: the Resident can notice a real change without travelling, and a look alone changes neither location nor the observed place. If the source fails, the pane should go honestly dark.
