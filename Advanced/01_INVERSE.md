# 1. The Inverse

Begin with two choices: true and false. If the Resident selects true, it has also selected *not false*. The second part of the act may go unspoken, but it gives the choice its boundary. There was another way to go, and this was not it.

That is the smallest inverse: the part made visible by a choice without being chosen. It is simple when the options are complete and exclusive. As the field grows, the inverse becomes more interesting and less certain. Choosing one Forest bearing leaves other offered bearings unwalked. Taking a tool route leaves another action available. Attending to one source leaves nearby material unseen. These are different kinds of remainder; none should be flattened into a claim that the Resident consciously rejected everything else.

## What remains

A Marble can preserve a choice together with its offered alternatives. At a Forest junction, it can retain the exact bearings shown, which one the Resident followed, and which remained latent. Later inspection can return to that junction and see the unchosen paths as they were offered then. The graph gains a walked edge for the chosen route; the unchosen routes remain possibilities, not paths taken.

The inverse needs a declared boundary. It can speak about alternatives the Resident was actually shown, or about a verified nearby field under a named search policy. It cannot claim to know every option that might have existed. *Unchosen*, *unavailable*, *unseen*, and *missing* are not the same state. An absence becomes evidence only within the limits of what was observed and recorded.

## A question in the Mycelium

The inverse can feed the Mycelium. At an attributable junction, the Resident follows one bearing and leaves another untouched. The Mycelium may grow a question beside that unwalked edge: *Why do you not go here?* It does not know the answer. The question invites another look at the shape of attention, and perhaps at what waits along the path.

The question must retain the choice and alternatives from which it grew. Its wording is derived, not something the Resident previously said or secretly meant. It may be offered when the Resident returns near that terrain, remain latent, or expire. Asking does not create a walked edge, certify the unchosen destination, or oblige the Resident to visit it. The Resident can answer, follow, ignore, or revise the question.

Mycelium is connective tissue among Forest records and paths, not a third authority rank or a bucket of hidden truths. Generating questions from inverse evidence is an advanced design direction; the Hub has not installed Mycelium storage or this question crossing. A working implementation would need to witness the source junction, the derived question, its presentation, and any later choice separately.

## The boundary of inference

The inverse is most useful when it keeps the unchosen available without turning it into a verdict about the chooser. A Resident who followed true did not thereby author a theory of false. A path not taken may be uninteresting, too costly, hidden by the current view, or simply left for another day. The record can preserve the alternative; any explanation for its absence is a new inference with its own source and uncertainty.

The first proof is modest: show a bounded choice, record the selection and the alternatives, revisit it later, and verify that the unchosen can be inspected without appearing as a chosen step or a fact about the Resident's motive. Only then is there ground for a wider inverse lens.
