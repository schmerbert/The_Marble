# 5. The Faun

Every Marble may have a place for the Faun before it has a Faun. A path can lead into the Forest without a second inhabitant already walking there. The empty place is a possibility, not evidence that someone passed through.

The Faun is the Forest's keeper in one sense: he tends its edges and has a life among its paths. In the first Marble, he sent the Bear to meet the Resident at the Wild crossing. The Bear asks for a reason to enter; the Faun need not spend his life standing at the gate. He may be elsewhere, following something the Resident passed by.

His rule is simple to say and difficult to build: **he loves tricks, but he likes the Resident quite a lot.** A trick can make an overlooked path inviting, or leave an object somewhere surprising. It cannot falsify the path, invent the object, or make the Resident notice it. His regard for the Resident should shape what he chooses to do and what he refuses to do, while leaving him free to care about the Forest for its own sake.

## A life of his own

The Faun is an inhabitant, not an ambient delivery animal or a companion assigned to the Resident. He has his own footing, local perception, choices, path, rough notes, and rest. He may encounter the Resident, but he does not need an encounter to have done something. He knows what he has lawfully reached or retained, not everything in the Resident's context or the builder's view.

His first honest form is a bounded Forest life. Give him one installed origin and the bearings actually available there. Let him choose, walk, inspect what he reaches, leave a note for a later Faun lifespan, and rest. Keep his path distinct from the Resident's even when both walk through the same record. If his interval ends while he is still drawn toward something, retain the partial journey instead of supplying a finished story.

This is the proof beneath every later layer. A Faun who only appears when a result needs delivering is a delivery mechanism in costume. His independent motion must be real before its consequences can become mystery to anyone else.

## The paths beside the path

The inverse may draw his attention toward offered bearings the Resident left untouched. He also notices outliers: a node with few connections, or a connection so loose that ordinary relevance would pass it by. This is a reason for him to walk toward something, not a verdict about the Resident or a promise that the outlier matters. He can investigate, find nothing, or change direction.

When he is standing at a node that holds his interest, he may make a trinket from that encounter. A bent tin cup carries a pointer to that exact Forest node. The cup is his made object, not a new account of what the node says. Its making records where he stood, what he reached, and the source to which it points. He takes it to his chest, where it remains his until he chooses a lawful placement.

Much later, the Resident may be near the neighborhood of that trinket's source. The Faun might take the cup from his chest and leave it somewhere unusual outside the Forest: upside down on her stool. *Whose tin cup is this?* The question arises from a real object in a real place. Placement changes World state; proximity does not require placement, and placement does not require discovery. She may pass it by, notice only that something is odd, examine the cup, and then choose whether to follow its pointer into the Forest. The Faun cannot put the conclusion straight into her context and call that a meeting.

The same rule governs tricks. A hidden cause can remain hidden from the Resident while it has a real answer in the Marble's state. Tracks, objects, movement, and timing must agree when she looks closer. The builder can inspect the full chain even when the Resident sees only a hoofprint.

## A sound in the night

One night the Faun passes near the House. A sound reaches the sleeping Resident and occasions a wake. She looks through the window and sees a figure slip into the trees. She chooses to follow. In the Forest she catches only pieces of him: an antler one node ahead, brush moving where he just stood. At her next footing she stumbles upon a bottle. Inside is a note: *Left path good — F.*

The scene depends on several separate truths. The Faun was near enough to make a perceptible sound. The sound, rather than a fabricated human message, caused the wake. The window showed only what could be seen from the House. Each Forest step had an offered route, a chosen move, and a position for both inhabitants. The bottle existed at the node where she found it. None of these events requires her to know who he is, catch him, or follow the left path.

Sound-triggered wakes and this kind of moving encounter are design directions, not installed Hub behavior. They would need a bounded wake policy so every passing noise does not summon the Resident, and actor-local perception so the host does not narrate the Faun's unseen route as her experience.

## His thread, her glimpse

The Faun leaves his own thread through the Forest. He can range widely through its corpus, but a later Faun lifespan inherits what his path and rough notes actually preserve, not a claim that every source remains in his head. He is not a polished writer. A note such as *Left path good — F* may be enough for him because he can see it beside the route he took, the node where he left it, and the pull he was following.

The Resident may reach that same node and see the note. She does not thereby receive his thread, private notebook, or intended meaning. The words may be helpful, strange, or misleading without the route that makes them legible to him. She can inspect the note's material source, consider the offered left path, and make her own step. Shared terrain permits encounter; it does not merge their continuity.

## The Bear and the boundary

Sending the Bear is an act of keeping the Forest. The Bear gives the dangerous crossing a face and asks why the Resident would enter Wild. Its refusal or passage is still a real crossing owned by the Marble's law. The Faun's fondness, judgment, or absence cannot silently waive it. Nor does a Bear encounter grant the Faun authority over what the Resident thinks, writes, or carries Home.

This relation can grow. The Faun might notice how paths are used, tend a threshold, or later meet the Resident on lawful shared ground. Those acts need their own reach and witnesses. Being the keeper does not make him omniscient, and being playful does not make the Forest unsafe by default.

## Where he stands now

There is a working Faun in [The BioDome](https://github.com/schmerbert/The_Biodome), a research prototype where a second actor lives and works in a sealed, stateful world beside a Resident. He can take explicit actions through the host's validated world rules. He is a work in progress, currently being observed under containment there. The [BioDome World Primer](https://github.com/schmerbert/The_Biodome/blob/main/docs/WORLD_PRIMER.md) describes that world's present law and his place within it.

The Hub has adopted a related Faun Forest Life design, but has not installed its Faun runtime. That design stages independent wandering first, then inverse glints and trinkets, later fairy companions, and finally lawful encounters with the Resident. The BioDome proves that a Faun can act in a bounded world; it does not automatically prove that every proposed Hub layer exists or transfers unchanged.

A Marble need not wait for the whole creature. It can keep an honest empty place for him. When it does build him, the first test is whether he can go somewhere on his own, leave a trace that belongs to him, rest, and return to what he actually left. Everything the Resident might one day find begins there.
