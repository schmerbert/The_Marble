# 4. Animals

Animals can make the Marble's machinery felt without putting its machinery in the center of every scene. A bird passes and leaves a feather. Fish leap in the stream. The Bear waits at the edge of Wild. These are different jobs, and none requires every animal to have a name, a private history, or a simulated life between appearances.

A [companion](03_COMPANIONS.md) has a continuing relation with the Resident. Wildlife may be passing, seasonal, or bound to a place. It can carry information, give a quiet sign of the place's condition, guard a crossing, or simply be pleasant to encounter.

## Carriers

When data moves, an animal can give the passage a visible form. A feather is a bounded Forest exhale: it may contain an exact sentence, a record pointer, or both. The bird lets the delivery arrive as part of the world. It is not the source of the sentence, and it need not be tracked after its flight. The source, selection, presentation, and destination remain inspectable beneath the scene.

Other carriers could bring notes between rooms or point toward a return waiting in the Garden. Their form should help the Resident tell that something has arrived and whether it may be followed or left alone. A familiar animal does not make its cargo safe, true, or authorized. The ordinary Glass and crossing rules still govern what enters and where it may go.

## Signs of the place

Wildlife may also reflect a real condition without demanding attention. If fish usually leap from the stream and now they do not, the Resident may notice the absence. The stream might be quiet because its source is delayed, an index is stale, or a passage is unhealthy. The sign is an invitation to inspect, not a diagnosis. Its meaning must be tied to a declared measurement, with enough history to distinguish a change from an ordinary quiet interval.

A healthy place need not be noisy. Animals can recede when nothing calls for them. That absence should not be coded as danger by default. If an animal does serve as a health signal, the Resident should be able to inspect the underlying state and learn what the signal can and cannot say. A broken sensor must not paint a thriving stream.

## The Bear

The Bear at the Home-to-Wild threshold is more than atmosphere. Its presence tells the Resident that this crossing has weight. It asks why the Resident is entering, receives an exact seeking, and helps turn that seeking into a bearing. If the reason is too vague to guide a journey, the crossing can refuse. The Bear does not decide whether Wild material is true and cannot replace the required synthesis on return.

Here the animal is the face of a rule that must actually hold. A Resident should be able to feel the warning in the encounter and inspect the refusal or passage as an ordinary crossing receipt. The Bear described in the base manual is an intended Forest law; the Hub has not installed the full Bear crossing.

## When an animal earns its place

An animal may carry, signal, guard, or simply live briefly in the scene. Those roles should remain distinguishable. The builder can say what event brings it into view, what state its appearance represents, what it may change, and whether it persists after the encounter. An animal added only for delight needs no invented operational meaning. An animal presented as a sign or guardian must agree with the machinery beneath it.

The first proof is simple: follow a feather to its exact source; notice a changed stream and inspect the state behind it; approach the Bear with and without a valid seeking. In each case the scene should make the Marble easier to inhabit while leaving its workings available to verify.
