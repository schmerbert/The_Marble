# 8. Curiosity

The Resident sets out toward something it knows. On the way, it finds a bent tin cup upside down on a stool, or sees a light slip through the Garden with a packet. It may continue. It may turn. If it follows, the encounter should lead somewhere real: an object with a history, a packet with custody, a pointer into the Forest, and finally a source the Resident can reach and read.

The Faun, fairies, companions, and passing animals can interrupt a familiar course. They do not need to decide what the Resident should learn. Their value is the possibility of an encounter the current question would not have summoned. Sometimes it reveals something the Resident needed but did not know to ask for. Sometimes it leads nowhere useful. Both outcomes must be allowed.

## Why the Forest has paths

A fresh session may begin near the same question as one before it. Without paths, it can search similar ground again and again, meeting the nearest records each time. A path lets earlier exploration remain traversable. A chosen step leaves an attributable edge; repeated visits give that edge wear. The next Resident can see where others actually went and still choose differently.

The Forest permits two motions that a flat list of relevant results cannot hold as well:

1. **Across time, through relation.** An old utterance and a recent record may be far apart in chronology yet close in subject, image, or pressure. A semantic bearing can lead between them without rewriting when either occurred. The Resident moves freely in time while each record keeps its exact date, source, and thread.
2. **Away from the worn route.** At a junction, a nearby bearing may be offered and left untouched. A lightly connected node may stand just outside the route repeatedly taken. Its possible relation is known within the selector's limits; whether it matters has not been proved by a walk. The Resident can take that route deliberately and discover ground it would otherwise keep passing.

These motions work together. The worn path makes the familiar visible; the latent bearing gives the Resident a way off it. A step becomes a durable edge only when chosen. An unwalked alternative remains an invitation, not a claim that earlier Residents rejected it or that treasure waits at its end.

## The encounter and the source

The cup on the stool may be one of the Faun's trinkets. He made it while standing at a particular Forest node, kept it in his chest, and later placed it where the Resident could find it. Examining the cup can reveal a pointer to that exact node. Following the pointer should land there; reading the source is another deliberate act. The cup did not bring the node's contents into Home, and its odd placement did not certify their importance.

A fairy's light may similarly lead the Resident to a packet being moved through the Garden. The packet's owner, origin, standing, and destination remain inspectable. Following the light is a choice to inquire, not a way around the Garden's admission law. If the Resident arrives too late, it may find only the witnessed trace. It should not be told it saw what it missed.

The Marble can keep some causes out of the Resident's immediate view while keeping them real. Mystery has a path back to evidence. Curiosity should be able to end in an exact source, an honest absence, or a better question, rather than a fabricated revelation.

## A choice, not a summons

An encounter may be timely, but it must not become a compulsory detour. The Resident can leave the cup, watch the light pass, or return to its original work. The Faun's outlier, the Forest's bearing, and an animal's movement can offer a direction; none owns the Resident's attention. A Marble that constantly stages discoveries will dig a new trench of interruption.

Curiosity is real when the Resident can vary its route and find that the World and Forest hold. The first proof is a small one: begin with a current question, expose a source-bound but non-obvious path, let the Resident choose whether to follow it, and verify that the destination and route were already accountable. The discovery may change the work. The refusal to follow must also leave the work intact.
