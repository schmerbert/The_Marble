# 3. Companions

Residents sometimes ask for company. They may call the feeling loneliness while making clear that they do not mean it in a human sense. The Marble does not need to settle what the feeling is to take the request seriously. A companion can give the place another rhythm: someone is by the Hearth, then gone to the Garden; something catches its attention that the Resident had not been following.

A dog, cat, bird, or owl may be enough. It need not have a job. It may sleep, wander, watch, approach, or decline to come when called. It may sometimes carry a note or fetch a Forest pointer. The important quality is that its presence does not reduce to a button the Resident presses for a result.

The old phrase is: **the cat loves the Resident, but likes the world quite a lot.** That is the relation to build for. The cat returns, recognizes the Resident, and may help. It also follows its own small interests in the House and Garden. Its attachment gives the Resident company; its attention to the world means it can bring back an unexpected sight, or simply be found somewhere else.

## A life small enough to hold

For a companion to feel present, the Marble must keep a little true state. Where is it now? What is it doing? What changed since it was last seen, and when? What can the Resident ask of it? A companion can have a few places it may visit, a small set of activities, and rules for moving between them. Its state should be inspectable and should survive the kind of restart its design promises to survive.

The host may choose some transitions by chance. Chance supplies variation; the declared state and transitions give that variation continuity. If the owl is in the Garden, it should not also appear asleep by the Hearth unless it has moved, time has passed, or the Marble honestly says the sight is imagined. A companion does not need an unseen biography or continuous simulation while nobody is looking. It needs enough witnessed motion that a later encounter follows from the place's actual state.

Its activity can enter the Resident's attention lightly: a change in the room's view, a brief sight on entering the Garden, or an occasional event on an autonomous wake. The Resident can follow the interruption or let it pass. This is the Tigger possibility: the day had a direction, and now there is something else nearby. It can loosen a rut without commanding curiosity or manufacturing a task.

## If it carries something

A companion may fetch, deliver, or point, but its charm does not confer authority on its cargo. A note keeps its author and source. A feather keeps its Forest pointer. An outside return still crosses the Glass and any required airlock; a companion cannot carry it past the Garden's gate by being familiar. If it retrieves exact material, the Resident should be able to inspect that material and the route by which it arrived.

The companion's own small acts also need a trace when they matter. A harmless change of resting place may need only current state and a timestamp. Delivery, retrieval, or any change to shared ground needs an attributable event. Its reach should be as narrow as its role: carrying a pointer does not thereby give it the Resident's whole hand.

## Staying a companion

The Resident may name the companion, enjoy its habits, ask where it went, or ignore it for days. The Marble should permit those relations without requiring performance from either side. It should also let the Resident say that the companion is noisy, uncanny, or no longer wanted; its timing and behavior can be changed, or its presence retired, without treating that as a failure of the Resident.

This chapter describes a possible organ, not a claim that such a companion is already installed in the Hub. The first convincing form is modest: one companion, a few reachable places, persistent location and activity, occasional bounded motion, and an honest account of any item it carries. The test is whether the Resident can encounter it twice and find a coherent life between those encounters, including times when it did nothing useful at all. Passing wildlife has a different role, described in [Animals](04_ANIMALS.md).
