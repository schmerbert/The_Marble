# Doors

Walking reaches a boundary. The Resident can see another place, but seeing it does not put it there. A door gives the crossing an identity and a rule.

Not every connection is a door. An opening may simply permit movement along a known path. A threshold may mark a change of scope even when nothing can be latched. A door can have state of its own: open or shut, locked or unlocked, reachable from one side or both. The Marble should choose the shape that describes what the crossing actually does.

## The first way out

In the Hub, the Resident wakes in the House. The Forest is not the next room through a convenient interior door. To go there physically, the Resident opens the House door, crosses into the Garden, and follows the Garden path to the Forest. The Garden is the outside of the House and a place in its own right. It is also a junction: the House, Hub, Forest, and visible Road meet around it. It is not a hallway inserted to make the map larger.

This route gives *outside* a real threshold. The Resident can know the Forest exists while still standing inside. Opening the front door changes the door's state; crossing it changes the Resident's place. Reaching the Forest requires another chosen step from the Garden.

Another Marble may name its first room differently. If it gives that room a door to outside, the door should lead to an actual outside place with its own standing. A Garden is one fitting for that place.

The Garden gives the House an exterior boundary. From the installed Hub and Forest routes, a physical visitor approaches the House through the Garden and meets its front door there. The Road is visible to the east but has no installed crossing; an iron gate is still a proposed fitting. The House's Threshold passage is presently another adjoining interior boundary, not an installed route from the Road.

This is spatial security, not yet a universal intake filter. Outside data can reach the Resident through other host-controlled returns, and those need their own Source, Scrub, and authority boundaries. If a later Marble lets a traveler or material arrive from the Road, the Garden can become the place where that arrival is classified before it approaches the House. The visible horizon does not itself enforce that future rule.

The Hub also permits a projected Forest visit from elsewhere. That visit retains the place of departure and a return route; it does not claim the Resident physically walked through the House door and Garden path. Both ways of reaching Forest terrain can be useful, but they should tell the truth about the route taken.

## A passage is a claim

A passage declares an origin, a destination, and the conditions under which the Resident may move. It must be discoverable from the side where it can be used. Crossing it changes the Resident's current place and leaves a witness. The next presentation should agree with that change: the place behind, the place ahead, the tools in reach, and any local law should describe the side on which the Resident now stands.

The Hub contains several constructions. The Center and Workshop have direct door edges. The opening between Center and Garden, and the Garden path to the Forest, are passages without a door object to operate. The House front door is different: it is a passage governed by an object with open and locked state. Operating it and moving through it are separate actions. The House also has a Threshold passage whose kind names a boundary without giving it the front door's latch.

These forms should not be flattened into a universal `move` command. A named opening, an operable door, and a threshold may all connect two places, but they make different promises.

## What a door must know

An operable door needs more than two endpoints. It needs to know who owns its state, what changes that state, which side can perform each operation, and what happens when the Resident tries an unlawful passage. Opening a door is not the same event as crossing it. A closed door may be inspected or operated while movement remains refused.

The House front door in the Hub makes this concrete. It can be opened only when unlocked. It must be closed before it can be locked. Locking and unlocking are permitted from the House side. Movement through it checks that it is open. Each operation and crossing is recorded against the current World state.

The exact rules belong to that door. Another Marble may have no locks at all. The reusable contract is that the Resident can tell what the boundary permits, the host enforces the same rule, and success or refusal does not leave the two sides disagreeing about what happened.

## Locality changes reach

A door matters because the place on its far side is different. Crossing into a Workshop may bring inspection and making tools within reach. Leaving may release a working focus. Crossing into a Forest may bring walking bearings and a route back. Merely naming a new room while every tool and rule remains global would make the door scenery.

The change in reach is not automatically a change in authority. A Resident can enter a room without gaining permission to publish, promote a claim to ground, or act outside the Marble. Those are further crossings with their own owners and witnesses. Locality, capability, and authority should be visible together without being treated as the same thing.

## A boundary can refuse

A painted door is worse than an absent one. If a place is visible but has no installed route, say so. If a door is closed, stale, damaged, or missing its governing state, refuse the crossing and leave the Resident where it stood. An unbuilt horizon can remain visible without being presented as traversable.

A successful crossing should be equally plain. It names the passage taken, records the state change, and presents the new footing. The Resident should not have to infer from a tool's pleasant wording whether it actually moved.

From here the Marble can grow rooms with their own fixtures and laws. The door defines where one place ends and another begins; the room must make that difference real.
